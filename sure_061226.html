Cover Letter · Sure

The administration had left half the staff out of the training.

Not maliciously — the English-speaking administrators had planned to brief the Mandarin-speaking teachers separately, and didn't realize that excluding them had become a loss-of-face problem and, by the time I landed, a political one inside the school. I don't speak Mandarin. I had one day to fix it.

I asked two questions: did they have translators on staff, and could they find one spare laptop for each. They could. I rebuilt the three-day training overnight — taught the translators an overview of the platform first, in front of the whole room, so they became the bridge, then ran each lesson twice: once for the full group, pausing for the translation to land, then again together. By day two the translators were anticipating the material as I delivered it. We ran three hours long and finished to applause. No second specialist, no extra resources. A restructured plan and two questions asked at the right moment.

That is the part of implementation I'm good at — the moment the plan meets reality and someone has to keep the launch on track without dropping the rigor. Sure ships embedded insurance programs in weeks, not months, for brands and carriers where a slipped launch is visible all the way up. That speed only works if the person owning the partner program can absorb the surprises and keep Engineering unblocked. That's the job I've done for sixteen years.

The numbers: 180+ implementations across B2B SaaS and low-code platforms — 30+ enterprise SaaS launches at VenturEd, 150+ across a 400-school portfolio at inRESONANCE. Six standard and ten to twelve concurrent at peak. Roughly $2B in annual client revenue protected. End-to-end every time: requirements through go-live through the partnership that follows. I gather what the partner actually needs, translate it into something Engineering can build, flag scope and timeline risks before they become delays, and write recaps that keep executives aligned.

I don't have insurance domain depth yet. I've never needed to walk in with it — domain knowledge is what discovery is for, and I've ramped on a new vertical every few years for a career. What I bring is the discipline underneath: a bench-science background where the protocol is the difference between a result you can trust and one you can't, applied to launches that must move fast and still hold.

Sure says it leaves egos at the door and gets the job done. That's the environment in which I do my best work.
