Same stage, different room

Two post-sales role-play setups that look identical on a calendar invitation. Both sit after signature. Both are called a scoping session. One puts a client across the table; the other puts colleagues there. The questions that earn a high score in one are unsayable in the other — and a brief that never states which room you are in leaves the candidate to guess, then grades the guess.

Quadrant 2

Client ↔ Implementer

The scene

Contract signed, statement of work in hand. The implementer owns the room. Across the table: an executive sponsor, operational leads, technical staff. Every sentence is spoken to a counterparty.

What the session produces

Reconciled scope. Named owners on both sides. Acknowledged dependencies with the critical path made explicit. A meeting cadence. A working definition of done.

Quadrant 4

Implementation Team ↔ New Team Member

The scene

Contract signed, kickoff pending. Internal preparation with the delivery pod — technical configuration owner, ongoing relationship owner, whoever else sits in the model. Every sentence is spoken to colleagues.

What the session produces

Role boundaries for the client room. A discovery sequence. Agreed additions to the standard phase model. The internal gate that must clear before anyone commits a date.

Quadrant 2

Client ↔ Implementer

What it achieves as an interview exercise

Highest fidelity to the work itself.

Tests discovery discipline, the separation of a sponsor's stated goal from operational reality, and whether a candidate can assign accountability to a client without spending goodwill. It requires no internal knowledge of the hiring company, and product depth brackets with a single sentence: assume you are trained. The default choice when only one exercise can be run.

Quadrant 4

Implementation Team ↔ New Team Member

What it achieves as an interview exercise

Highest signal on how someone joins a team.

Tests role hygiene inside a pod, coachability, and the quality of questions asked when the honest position is I do not know your product yet. It is the only post-sales setup a candidate with no product exposure can occupy at full strength — which is precisely why a brief weighted toward internal-register prompts reads as this room.

One sentence closes the gap

Before a scoping role-play begins, the casting has to be said out loud: am I the vendor with you as the institution, or am I the new team member with you as the delivery team?

Content assumptions are what an exercise is meant to test — scope, data, governance, change management, what done means. Assumptions about the room are not. Those are a logistics fact, available for the asking, and expensive to guess. A candidate who prepares brilliantly for the wrong room has demonstrated everything except the one question that would have placed them in the right one.
