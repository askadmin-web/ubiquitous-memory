Implementation Scenario — Cathy Rudolph

Tesseract College
Undergraduate admissions live by August 15.

How I'd approach a CRM implementation with a hard launch date, sparse sales notes, and client leaders who don't agree on what success means. The method behind it is on How I Run Implementations.

Tesseract College and everyone in it are invented. The pressures are the ones real implementations bring.

The situation

A private nonprofit college has just signed. Phase 1 is undergraduate admissions: CRM, application management, and events, plus a knowledge-base assistant for routine family questions. Its Vocational division, in a second city, is in the contract as a "next phase," with no date.

The college's SIS vendor also sponsors the Vocational division, and it's upgrading the SIS this spring. Once that upgrade lands, the SIS fields are locked, and the CRM has to fit them. The one IT contact is split across in-house work, the upgrade, and this project.

Hard dates
June 30Legacy CRM shuts off. Data migrated and validated before then.
August 15Fall applications open.
Where it gets hard

The executive sponsor, the undergraduate director, and the Vocational director each define success differently, and the SIS vendor has its own stake in the timing. None of that is in the sales notes. Discovery is where it surfaces.

Discovery — the questions that change the plan
  1. What does success look like a year from now, in numbers?
  2. Who decides when the admissions offices disagree?
  3. What has Vocational been told about timing?
  4. How clean is the legacy data, and who owns it?
  5. What else does IT have on its plate before August?
  6. Which workflows must work on day one, and which can wait?
  7. Who uses the system daily, and how were they trained last time?
  8. What should the assistant answer, and who approves its content?
